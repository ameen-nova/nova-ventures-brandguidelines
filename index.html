Logo system

Our logo is the most visible expression of the brand. A faceted blue-and-silver star stands for clarity, progress and global opportunity. Always use the supplied master artwork; never redraw or retype it.

The mark

A star in motion

  • 1
    Blue faceted pointsThe upper facets carry Nova Blue and Sky Blue: confidence and direction.
  • 2
    Silver sweepThe lower arc moves from lower-left to upper-right, the same forward direction used by the shard.
  • 3
    Dimensional planesLight and shadow across the facets create depth. That rendering belongs to the logo alone; everything around it stays flat.

Logo hierarchy

Dimensional in colour, flat for single colour

The artwork is fixed; what we decide is which version is used. The dimensional logo is used wherever colour is available. The flat white and black stars cover single-colour work, so the brand never depends on rendering to look right.

1 · Signature · DimensionalCovers, signage, website hero, video, title slides and event backdrops, at generous sizes. The only place metallic shading appears.
2 · Single colour · Flat white or blackFoil, embossing, stamps, embroidery, engraving and one-colour print.

Protecting the artwork

Three rules this logo needs

The logo’s fine silver shading and thin wordmark are part of its character. These rules keep them visible.

Small sizes

Use the full-colour symbol from 48 px up. For favicons and app icons, use the pixel-tuned files in 05 Digital, never a scaled-down logo. For single-colour work under 48 px (stamps, embroidery, foil), use the Flat symbol.

White backgrounds

The silver sweep is pale (1.8:1 against white). On pure white keep the horizontal logo at least 170 px or 40 mm wide. For smaller placements, put Cloud or Light Grey behind it.

The only metallic element

The dimensional logo is the one place metallic shading appears, and it is reserved for signature moments. The shard, icons and interface stay flat, so the logo keeps its shine. Never add bevels, glows or shadows to it.

Colour proportions

Mostly light, anchored by navy, signed in blue

Nova Blue is the signature, not the backdrop: the star, buttons, links and one highlight per headline. Navy and charcoal carry authority. Deep Blue, Sky Blue and Silver are supporting tones for hover states, charts and shard facets, never the main colour of a layout. Dark applications can invert the balance, with navy dominant.

60%White & light grey
25%Navy & charcoal
10%Nova Blue
5%Accents

The last 5% covers Silver, Sky Blue, Deep Blue and the Hot Pink accent, which stays around 2% on its own. Use no more than two blues in one composition.

Accent colour

Hot Pink: seen, not noticed

One warm accent runs quietly through the whole brand. Pink has one job: it marks the point. It shows where a message starts, where you are, what matters and where Nova signs off, always as a small mark and never as an area. Visible enough to create distinction, restrained enough to never overpower the brand. It is never part of the logo.

How much

  • Up to two pink moments in a layout, each doing a different job. Many pieces need only one.
  • Always a mark, never an area: if the pink is the biggest thing or the first thing you notice, take some out.
  • If a pink element doesn’t do one of the four jobs, it doesn’t belong.

Where it stays out

  • The logo, in every version.
  • Buttons, links, body text and icons.
  • Backgrounds, large shapes and photography.
  • Gradients, or pink mixed with blue inside one shard.

Typography rules

  • Set headlines in Arsenal Regular, in sentence case, with tight line spacing (1.03–1.18). Never all caps, never bold.
  • Use Arsenal at 28 px and above only. Smaller headings use Lato Bold.
  • Highlight one key phrase per headline in Nova Blue, or now and then in Hot Pink. Never more than one.
  • Body and lead text in Lato Regular, Slate or Charcoal, 16 px minimum on screen, 50–75 characters per line.
  • Buttons: Lato Bold, 6 px corners, one primary per view. Full spec in Buttons below.
  • Left-align text. Centre only short, single statements.
  • Where the brand fonts are unavailable (e.g. Outlook), use Trebuchet MS for headlines and Arial for text. Arsenal is a light humanist sans-serif, so never swap in a serif.

05

Depth & surfaces

Depth comes from composition, not rendering: overlap, scale, white space, tone and real photography. Surfaces are flat by default. The dimensional logo is the only rendered element in the system; everything around it stays flat, so the logo keeps its presence. The one exception is a single glass element: a panel over a photograph, or one glass shard.

Where depth comes from

Four ways to build depth without effects

If an element needs gradients and shadows to look good, the layout needs work. Reach for these first.

OverlapTwo flat shards in different tones, one over the other.
ScaleA large cropped shard against a small one.
SpaceGenerous white space around a single element.
ToneLight and dark surfaces set against each other.

06

Brand graphics: The Shard System

Nova Ventures’ graphic language is built from one element drawn from the star. The shard stands for the individual elements of a business journey: formation, licensing, visas, compliance and ongoing support. Brought together through controlled arrangement, overlap, rotation and cropping, they form one structured whole and move in one clear direction.

The Shard System

Different elements. One clear direction.

Core principles

What the shards do, and why it matters to Nova

A flexible graphic language built from one distinctive element, where individual forms come together to create a coherent whole.

01 · Structure

Individual shards are distinct elements; their arrangement creates one organised composition.

Nova brings separate business requirements together through a structured approach.

02 · Direction

The asymmetrical, tapered form introduces movement and progression, without becoming an arrow.

Nova moves clients from first requirements to an established business.

03 · Connection

Shards interact through proximity, alignment and controlled overlap.

Services, people and processes work together toward one business objective.

Point of origin

From the star, to the shard, to the system

The shard is not invented decoration. It takes the angle and taper of the star’s sweep, so the wider visual language always leads back to the logo the client chose.

The starThe overarching identity.
The shardOne element, drawn from the star.
The systemMany shards, one flexible composition.

Construction

Built on the star’s sweep

The base shard sits on the same 24° line as the star’s sweep, tip to tip. Every shard in the system is this one shape: never redrawn, stretched or given extra facets.

Anchor points
Four: two tips and two shoulders, set just off-centre so the form is asymmetrical.
Facets
Split tip to tip. The upper facet is the lighter tone, the lower facet one step darker.
Proportion
Length 100 : width 18, fixed.
Orientation
Tip up and to the right at 24°, the angle of the star’s sweep.

Master files: Nova Ventures Logo Package/06 Shard. Build every composition from these files, never from a traced or redrawn shape.

Variations

Five controlled uses of one shape

Every variation comes from the same base shard. A variation is a way of placing the shard, not permission to invent a new shape.

PrimaryThe base shard on the sweep angle. Upper facet light, lower facet dark.
RotatedTurned to suit a composition, never to point at content like an arrow.
FlippedMirrored for balance; the dark facet moves to the top. Use sparingly.
CroppedRunning off the edge with enough of the form left to read.
ExtendedTwo shards on one line with a controlled overlap, as a divider or accent.

Colour

Two flat facets, one tone apart

Each shard pairs two neighbouring palette tones split on the facet line, which gives depth without rendering. Pale tints are single flat colours for quiet background layers. Never a gradient inside a shard.

Glass shard

About one shard in ten can be glass

Glass is the exception, never the system. Across a series, roughly one shard in ten is glass; in any one layout, at most one, and it is never the largest. It only works where there is something to see through: on a photograph or a dark surface. On white and near-white surfaces it washes out, so the shard stays flat.

✓ On a photographThe skyline shows through. Clear of the face; the large shard stays flat.
✓ On Midnight or NavyOverlapping a flat shard, so the glass has something to show. Pink stays flat.
✕ On White, Cloud or Light GreyNothing to see through: the glass turns into a washed-out tint. Use a flat shard.

With photography

Frame the photo, don’t filter it

  • The shard may crop, frame or sit beside a photograph.
  • Keep it clear of faces and key information in the image.
  • Keep shards solid; one supporting shard may be glass. Never metallic.
  • Let the photograph keep its natural colour and depth.

✓Do

  • ✓Build every shard from the base shard.
  • ✓Keep the slim, asymmetrical silhouette and fixed proportions.
  • ✓Use flat palette tones, split on the facet line. Glass for about one shard in ten, never on white.
  • ✓Crop with intention and leave generous negative space.
  • ✓Use it selectively, to support the content.

  • ✕Invent new polygons or turn the shard into an arrow or chevron.

  • ✕Arrange shards into a star, scatter them randomly, or pack them into dense tiles that read as a sports or tech brand.

Creative direction

This is direction, not a rulebook. Start from an idea, not a layout: find the one thing the piece has to say, pick a way in, then design something that fits that message. It applies to everything Nova makes, from posts and posters to decks, web pages and video. The brand stays recognisable through a few constants, listed under the examples; everything else is a creative choice.

The feel

Clear, confident, calm, forward

Two pieces in a row should not look the same.

Clear

One idea per piece, said plainly. If it needs a second look to understand, simplify it.

Confident

Big type, bold crops, real space. Nothing apologetic, nothing shouting.

Calm

Light surfaces, natural photography, few elements. Premium consultancy, not a sale.

Forward

Movement on the 24° sweep: from the first question to an established business.

Ways in

Six starting points for an idea

Use them to get unstuck, combine them, or ignore them when a better idea comes along.

The question

Lead with what the client is actually asking (“Mainland or free zone?”) and let the type fill the frame.

The steps

Show the journey as separate pieces coming together: shards, numbered stages, a carousel that moves.

The person

A real client or advisor at the moment it clicks. The outcome, told through a face and their own words.

The place

The UAE as opportunity: a street, an office, a skyline, photographed honestly in daylight.

The detail

A close crop of something tangible: a signed licence, office keys, the first business card.

The fact

One verified figure or date, set very large, with its source. Only facts Nova can check.

Examples, not templates

What keeps it Nova

  • The supplied logo file, in the version that suits the background, with room around it. It signs off rather than leads: about 10% of the canvas width, on its own, with nothing set beside it.
  • Arsenal for headlines, Lato for everything else, with one phrase picked out in blue (or, now and then, Hot Pink).
  • A quiet touch of Hot Pink, the accent you see but don’t notice.
  • Colours from the palette, mostly light, with blue used as the signature rather than the backdrop.
  • Graphics drawn from the shard, kept flat. The logo is the only shiny thing.
  • Content that is true and checkable.

Where to explore

  • Layout and composition: type-led, photo-led, split, graphic-led, or something new.
  • Scale and contrast: a headline that fills the frame, or one small line in a lot of space.
  • Cropping: full-bleed photos, shards cut hard off the edge, shards that frame or slice a photo.
  • Series and rhythm: alternate light and dark, let a shard travel across carousel slides or a campaign.
  • Motion: shards sliding in along the 24° line, type that builds word by word.
  • Format and medium: posts, stories, banners, print, signage, video.

Working with AI tools

Image generators redraw the logo, replace Arsenal and Lato with lookalikes and invent glossy 3D shapes, so the result looks machine-made and off brand. When an AI tool helps with a poster, banner or post, it builds the layout from the files in this package and follows this guide. Generated images are only acceptable as photography, and only when they meet the photography rules. For AI tools, the same rules are written as plain text in BRAND.md, with ready-to-use sections for image generators, video generators, coding agents, Canva and Figma.

The rule

Assemble from real files, never generate the design

✓Do

  • ✓Place the real logo file from the logo package, in the version that suits the background, at about 10% of the width.
  • ✓Set headlines in Arsenal Regular and text in Lato, from the supplied font files.
  • ✓Build graphics from the shard master files: flat two-tone, on the 24° sweep, up to four shards, cropped off the edge.
  • ✓Use palette colours only, in the 60 / 25 / 10 / 5 balance.
  • ✓Keep one headline and one clear action: the brand button (Midnight, 6 px corners, sentence case) or a text link. Change the layout freely.
  • ✓Check every fact, figure and contact detail before publishing.

✕Don’t

  • ✕Generate, redraw or retype the logo or the star.
  • ✕Use 3D, glossy or extruded stars, lens flares, light rays or blue haze, or glass beyond one panel over a photo or one supporting shard.
  • ✕Substitute other fonts (Arsenal is a sans-serif, never a serif), or use Arsenal Bold.
  • ✕Add checkmarks in circles, icon bubbles, emoji or boxed text cards.
  • ✕Use all-caps or pill-shaped buttons, gradients or drop shadows.
  • ✕Let the tool invent statistics, prices, awards or contact details.
